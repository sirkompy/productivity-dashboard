# Social Comment Mining

## Purpose

Use comments as qualitative evidence, not as a generic sentiment score.

## Best use

Prioritize comments under:
- creator outliers
- posts teaching content strategy
- controversial or highly shared posts
- posts relevant to B2B growth, GTM, AI, fintech/stablecoins or distribution

## Capture

Preserve when publicly available:
- comment text
- timestamp
- likes/upvotes
- source post URL
- reply context where useful

Avoid collecting unnecessary personal details.

## Classify

Useful buckets:
- questions
- agreement/praise
- objections
- confusion
- counterarguments
- requests
- practical intent
- debate
- jokes/culture signals

## Analyze

Cluster repeated themes and distinguish:
- repeated pattern
- high-intensity minority
- one loud comment

Useful questions:
- What part did people quote/repeat?
- What did they disagree with?
- What questions remain unanswered?
- What wording does the audience naturally use?
- Did the comments reveal a stronger follow-up idea than the original post?

## Output

- sample size + confidence
- top themes
- audience questions
- objections/counterarguments
- exact short language snippets when useful
- implications for why the post resonated
- follow-up content opportunities

Do not infer broad market sentiment from one comment section.
